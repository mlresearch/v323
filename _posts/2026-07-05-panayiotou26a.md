abstract: Causal discovery is challenging in general dynamical systems because, without
  strong structural assumptions, the underlying causal graph may not be identifiable
  even from interventional data. However, many real-world systems exhibit directional,
  cascade-like structure, in which components activate sequentially and upstream failures
  suppress downstream effects. We study causal discovery in such chain-reaction systems
  and show that the causal structure is uniquely identifiable from blocking interventions
  that prevent individual components from activating. We propose a minimal estimator
  with finite-sample guarantees, achieving exponential error decay and logarithmic
  sample complexity. Experiments on synthetic models and diverse chain-reaction environments
  demonstrate reliable recovery from a few interventions, while observational heuristics
  fail in regimes with delayed or overlapping causal effects.
